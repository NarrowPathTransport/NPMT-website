Common questions

Straight answers

Tap a question to see the answer. If yours isn’t here, give us a call. You’ll get a straight answer.

Booking a trip

How do I schedule a ride?

Request a ride online, call us at (513) 463-7284, or email russ@narrowpathtransport.com with the appointment date, time, pickup address, and destination. We’ll confirm in writing with the pickup window and the price.

How far in advance should I book?

As soon as the appointment is set. Twenty-four to forty-eight hours ahead is ideal. For standing appointments, set them up once and we’ll keep them on the calendar.

Do you take same-day requests?

Yes, during our hours and when the schedule truly allows it. If it doesn’t, we’ll tell you no right away instead of giving you a maybe that turns into a no-show.

Do I need a doctor’s order to ride?

No. Private clients can book with us directly. Facilities just follow whatever approval process they already use.

Can I book a round trip?

Yes. We can come back at a set time, or come back when you call to say you’re done. If someone needs to stay on site during a procedure, we also offer wait and return.

On the day

Will the driver help me into the building?

Yes. We provide door-through-door help: from inside the home to the vehicle, and from the vehicle into the appointment. We don’t just drop you at the curb or a sliding door and leave you to find your way.

Can a family member or caregiver ride along?

Yes. One companion rides free on every trip. Just let us know when you book so we can plan the seating.

What happens if my appointment runs late?

Call us and we’ll adjust. Appointments run over all the time. It’s normal, and there’s nothing to apologize for.

What if the driver is running behind?

You’ll hear from us before you have to ask. A late driver is frustrating. A late driver nobody warned you about is what really breaks trust, and we take that seriously.

Will I be sharing the vehicle with other passengers?

No. Every trip is private. No shared rides, no detours to pick someone else up, and no waiting in the vehicle while a stranger finishes an appointment.

Wheelchairs and mobility

Do I have to transfer out of my wheelchair?

No. You stay in your own chair the whole trip, tied down at four points, plus a lap and shoulder belt.

Do you take power wheelchairs?

Yes, as long as the chair fits the rated capacity of our vehicle and tie-down system. Tell us the make and model when you book so we can confirm it fits ahead of time, not in your driveway.

Can I bring my walker, oxygen or other equipment?

Walkers, canes, and other personal mobility equipment ride with you and get handed back to you at the door. If you use portable oxygen, let us know when you book so we can plan for it.

Do you provide stretcher or gurney transport?

No. Stretcher service takes separate licensing, equipment, and a second crew member, and we won’t take a trip we aren’t equipped to run safely.

Cost and payment

How much does a ride cost?

Every trip gets its own price before it’s booked: a base rate plus loaded miles, which are the miles you’re actually in the vehicle. You’ll have the price in writing before you commit. Call us or send the pickup address and destination, and we’ll price it for you.

Do you accept Medicaid or insurance?

No. We’re private pay and facility direct. Some clients get reimbursed through a long-term care policy or a veterans benefit, and we’re glad to provide paperwork for that, but we don’t bill insurance or transportation brokers directly.

Am I charged for the drive to reach me?

No. You only pay for loaded miles.

What forms of payment do you take?

Private clients pay by card or electronic payment. Facilities are invoiced directly on agreed terms.

Where and when we run

What areas do you serve?

Four Ohio counties: Hamilton, Butler, Warren, and Clermont. That includes Cincinnati, Norwood, Blue Ash, Hamilton, Fairfield, West Chester, Middletown, Lebanon, Mason, Springboro, Batavia, Milford, Amelia, and more.

Do you cross into Kentucky or Indiana?

No. We only run trips within Ohio. Crossing a state line counts as interstate travel, which is outside our current operating authority, so we can’t take those trips.

What days do you operate?

Seven days a week, Monday through Sunday, 6:00 AM to 4:00 PM.

Can you cover a full dialysis schedule?

Usually, yes. Standing dialysis runs Monday, Wednesday, Friday or Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, and we run on all of those days. Our hours end at 4:00 PM, so a late chair time or a long ride home may not fit. We’ll tell you that up front instead of letting you find out when a chair time gets missed.

Drivers, safety and trust

Are your drivers trained and screened?

Yes. We check driving records before hiring, run background checks and drug tests, and document training on wheelchair securement, safe loading, and passenger assistance. Training is written down and signed off, not assumed.

What if the driver is tired?

Then the trip gets moved, reassigned, or turned down. We have a written fatigue policy, and we follow it. No one drives a passenger while too tired, no matter how busy the schedule is.

How do you keep the vehicles clean and safe?

We follow a written maintenance program, document inspections before and after every trip, and clean interiors before the day starts, not whenever someone gets around to it. We treat working heat and air conditioning as safety equipment, not extras.

What makes you different from other companies?

Everyone in this business says they care. What we do differently is put in writing what we won’t do, tell you when we can’t cover something, and give you the price before you commit. Try us for a few trips before you trust us with anything important.

Is this an ambulance service?

No. We only provide non-emergency transport, and we don’t respond to emergencies of any kind. If it’s an emergency, call 911.

Still have a question?

Ask us. You’ll get a real answer.

We’d rather spend ten minutes on the phone now than have you find out something important on the day of the appointment.
